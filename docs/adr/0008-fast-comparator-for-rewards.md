# ADR-0008: Fast comparator for rewards, official execution match for reported numbers

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-14

## Context

GRPO scores every completion by running it, hundreds of thousands of times over a run, so
the reward's checker has to be cheap and safe. Reported accuracy has to be comparable with
published Spider numbers, which come from the official test-suite evaluator
(`taoyds/test-suite-sql-eval`, vendored as `test_suite_eval`, ADR-0003). The evaluator
does more than compare results: it removes every `DISTINCT` from both queries before
running them, decides that row order counts when `order by` appears anywhere in the gold
query, opens a fresh connection per query, and calls `asyncio.run` for each one, which
fails inside a notebook's event loop. If the reward and the reported metric disagree too
often, training optimizes something other than what is reported.

## Decision

- **Rewards** use the fast comparator: `forge.sql.compare.results_match` on results from
  `forge.sql.executor` (FG-106, FG-107). It compares the rows the two queries return:
  multisets, or lists under a top-level `ORDER BY`, numbers by value with floats rounded
  to 6 decimals, and reordered columns up to 6 wide.
- **Reported numbers** use official execution match: `forge.eval.testsuite.
  official_exec_match`, which calls the vendored `eval_exec_match` directly with
  `plug_value`, `keep_distinct` and the progress bar off. It skips `evaluate()`, which
  rewrites `value` in predictions (upstream issue #21). Predictions that are empty,
  comment-only or that the executor denies are false without running (upstream issue
  #33). Each item runs in a sandbox worker process that is killed when the item takes
  longer than `item_timeout_s` (60 s by default; a killed item is false), under one
  overall deadline (ADR-0014).
- **The eval runner bounds official EX tighter than upstream** (FG-202, decided after the
  Phase 1 gate). `forge eval run` calls `official_exec_match` with `item_timeout_s=10`,
  and a prediction whose sandboxed run timed out at 2,000 ms is no match without going
  to the evaluator at all. Upstream gives each query 60 s. At 60 s, one adversarial
  prediction could hold an item for a minute while its worker's memory grows with no
  limit (ADR-0014, Known limits), and a batch of them could take hours. Most of Spider's
  gold queries run in milliseconds and the slowest, a training one, in 1.2 s on the
  laptop, so 10 s for the whole item cuts off no correct prediction with Spider's one
  database per folder. The price is a correct prediction slower than 2 s in the executor,
  or an item slower than 10 s, which counts as wrong here and might pass upstream.
  `tests/integration/test_eval_runner.py::test_official_ex_bounds` checks both bounds.
- **Agreement is measured, not assumed.** `forge eval agreement` mutates gold queries
  with sqlglot (`forge.eval.mutations`), scores each prediction with both checkers and
  writes the rate and the first 20 distinct disagreements to
  `docs/audit/agreement-<date>.md`. Invariant I5 requires 98% over all items; it runs on
  fixture mutations in CI and on 2,000 val mutations wherever the full data is present.
- The report gives every count for distinct items too. Items repeat: unchanged gold
  queries come back on every pass over the questions, and questions that share a gold
  query can get the same mutation. An item with the database, gold and prediction of an
  earlier one is the same check again.
- `forge eval gold --split X` gives every gold query to both checkers as its own
  prediction; anything under 100% fails.

## Measured

[`docs/audit/agreement-2026-11-15.md`](../audit/agreement-2026-11-15.md), generated by
`forge eval agreement --mutations 2000 --split val --seed 0`: the checkers agree on
99.15% of 2,000 items (1,983), and on 98.96% of the 1,445 distinct items (1,430). Gold
scores 100% on val (872 of 872) with both.

All 17 disagreeing items, 15 of them distinct, come from the evaluator removing
`DISTINCT`:

- 14 items (13 distinct) have a `COUNT(DISTINCT x)` gold. The evaluator runs `count(x)`,
  so a prediction that counts the distinct values of another column with no NULLs gets
  the same row count and "matches". The fast comparator says no, which is right: the
  answers differ.
- 3 items (2 distinct) have a `SELECT DISTINCT` gold, and the prediction returns the same
  distinct rows with other duplicates underneath. The fast comparator says yes, because
  the results are equal; the evaluator compares the rows without `DISTINCT` and says no.

None came from `ORDER BY` detection, empty results, float rounding or column orders.
The fast comparator is left as it is. In the first case it is the stricter and correct
one; in the second the two read the same results differently, and copying the
evaluator would mean rewriting predictions before they run.

## Consequences

- Rewards can disagree with reported numbers on `DISTINCT`, so a reward-hacking audit
  (FG-601) should look for gains that the official metric doesn't show.
- The mutations never add or drop a `DISTINCT`, which model outputs often do, and where
  the evaluator is lenient on purpose. FG-307 re-checks I5 on real baseline outputs,
  which will measure that case.
- Two checkers must be kept in step: a change to `forge.sql.compare` reruns the agreement
  check and commits a new report.
- Reported EX from `forge eval run` is upstream's verdict within those two bounds, and
  the run's manifest records them (`execution.timeout_ms`,
  `execution.official_item_timeout_s`), so a number made under other bounds can't pass
  for one made under these.

## Revisit if

Agreement on real model outputs (FG-307) falls under 98%, or `DISTINCT` cases make up
a large share of the disagreements there. Also if a correct prediction is ever cut off
by the 2 s or 10 s bound, and when the test-suite databases land (FG-508): an item then
runs gold and the prediction on every database in the folder, so its 10 s must scale
with their number (ADR-0014).

Each disagreement gets its stored verdict when scored again, and each one goes away once
the evaluator keeps `DISTINCT`. None comes from anything else, so the fast comparator has
no fault here for GRPO's reward to inherit. The reference writes `DISTINCT` more often than
the candidates (the replies column), and its dev run, with the most such replies, is the
one run that falls short on its own.

The product owner's decision, on these numbers:

- I5 is plan section 4's "fast comparator agreement with the official evaluator is ≥ 98%
  on stored baseline outputs", measured over FG-307's baseline outputs pooled: the table's
  last row.
- Each run of the chosen model, `Qwen/Qwen2.5-Coder-1.5B-Instruct` (ADR-0012: val with p1
  and with p2, the I6 repeat and dev), must also reach 98% on its own, since GRPO's reward
  stands in for official EX on that model's outputs. All four do. A run of another model
  may fall short alone, as the reference's dev run does.
- Every committed run's agreement is still reported: each run's report counts where its
  checkers disagree, and the test names every committed run's rate when it fails.
- Official EX keeps the evaluator's default, `keep_distinct` off, so reported numbers stay
  comparable with published Spider numbers. `official_exec_match(keep_distinct=True)` is
  for this check only.
- `tests/integration/test_invariants.py::test_i5_agreement_on_committed_runs` checks I5 so,
  and plan section 4's table stays as it is.

The first consequence above holds on real outputs: rewards and reported numbers part on
`DISTINCT`, so FG-601's audit should look for fast EX gains that official EX doesn't show,
a trained model adding or dropping `DISTINCT` among them.
